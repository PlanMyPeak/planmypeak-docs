Plan gap alerts

Plan gap alerts

Compliance alerts look backwards — did the athlete do what you prescribed? Plan gap alerts look forwards: they tell you when an athlete’s calendar is empty for an upcoming week, while you still have time to write it.

When you get one

The check runs once a week. If an athlete has no workouts scheduled for the week after next, you get one alert covering every athlete in that situation.

The alert always covers a whole calendar week, Monday to Sunday, in the athlete’s own time zone. It is never a rolling “next 7 to 14 days” window, because half of one week and half of another isn’t something you can sit down and plan.

What counts as a gap

A gap means zero scheduled workouts in that week. A single scheduled session — even a recovery spin or a rest day you’ve written in — is enough for the athlete not to be flagged.

You only hear about each athlete once

This is the important part. Once an athlete appears in an alert, they will not appear again — no matter how many weeks pass — until their calendar changes. You are told about a gap, not reminded about it weekly.

Repeating the check also leaves that athlete out of new emails and in-app notifications until the alert clears.

The alert clears when you add a new workout on or after the Monday of the week you were told about. That’s the signal that you’ve planned forward past the gap. Workouts already present when the alert was sent do not clear it. From then on the athlete is watched again, and if their plan later runs out, you’ll hear about it once more.

Editing the current week doesn’t clear it. If you were told that the week of 24 August is empty and you add a session to this week, the gap you were told about is still there, so the alert stays quiet rather than repeating itself.

If you plan one week at a time as a matter of habit, you’ll still hear about the same athletes fairly often — each time you plan forward the alert clears, and each time the newly-upcoming week is empty it opens again. That’s a real gap each time, not a repeat. If it isn’t useful for how you work, a head coach can switch the alert off for the whole organization.

Who gets flagged

Every active athlete with a coach assigned, in an organization that has the alert turned on.

That includes athletes with nothing scheduled at all — no plan this week, next week, or the week after. You’ll hear about them once, which is usually worth knowing, and then not again until someone plans for them.

Athletes who are deactivated, or who don’t have an active coach assigned, are never included.

What the alert looks like

You get one notification and one email per check — not one per athlete. The in-app notification previews up to three athlete names and the full count. The email lists affected athletes, most urgent first, subject to the size limit below.

An athlete is treated as more urgent when next week is also empty, which means their plan runs out within days rather than weeks. Each athlete’s line tells you which case they’re in:

  • “Planned through next week, then nothing.”
  • “Planned through this week only (3 sessions), then nothing.”
  • “Nothing planned at all — this week, next week, or the week after.”

Each athlete also shows their plan type (custom or off-the-shelf) and the name of their currently detected plan, when available. If their plan type is unset or no current plan has been detected, the email says so. When the detected plan is available, select its name to open the training plan.

Athlete names link straight to their profile, so you can go from the email to planning their week in one click.

Training context

The email shows up to eight detailed athlete cards. Each card includes the plan type, detected plan name and end date when available, and recent training context:

  • Cycling compliance over four weeks: completed cycling TSS divided by planned cycling TSS across the last four completed Monday-Sunday weeks in the athlete’s timezone. The current partial week is excluded. Percentages are rounded down to whole numbers and can exceed 100%. Green means at least 90%, amber means at least 70%, and red means below 70%.
  • Four-week cycling TSS: completed and planned load, with a weekly bar chart. These figures use stored synced activities and include cycling only.
  • Fitness (CTL, all sports): fitness at the close of the last completed week, alongside its change from exactly 28 days earlier. Unlike cycling TSS, CTL includes load from all sports. The card shows the comparison dates.

The bars scale to each athlete’s highest planned week. Completed bars stop at the full chart width even when completed load is higher. If there is no planned load, bars scale to the highest completed week instead.

If more athletes are affected, their names appear in a compact list linking to their profiles. Large lists can show fewer detailed cards, or names only, to keep the email readable within its size limit. If even the names cannot all fit, the email states how many additional athletes were omitted.

Missing data

A dash (—) means a metric is unavailable. A failed history read makes all four cycling load rows and their totals unavailable for that athlete; available fitness can still appear. Fitness or its comparison can be unavailable when there is not enough ready history.

Missing TSS on an individual workout contributes zero, matching the weekly review. Empty stored weeks also count as zero, including weeks before activation or the first recorded activity. These are totals of stored synced activities, not a claim that every past workout has been imported.

If the last four weeks have no planned or completed cycling TSS, the card says No cycling TSS in the last 4 weeks, shows zero cycling totals and omits bars. Compliance is a dash when planned cycling TSS is zero. Runs and swims can still contribute to all-sport CTL.

Turning it off

There are two levels:

  • Just the emails, for you. Go to Settings → Notifications and turn off Plan gap alerts, then select Save changes. The email footer links directly to the Notifications section in Settings. You’ll still see the in-app notification, so nothing is lost — it just stops arriving in your inbox.
  • The whole alert, for the organization. A head coach can switch plan gap alerts off for everyone. Nobody in the organization receives them, in-app or by email.

Why an athlete might be flagged unexpectedly

Scheduled workouts reach PlanMyPeak from TrainingPeaks, and there is a short delay between you writing a week and it appearing here. If you’ve just planned a week and an alert still names that athlete, the sync hadn’t caught up when the check ran. Adding any workout from that week onwards clears the alert, so the athlete won’t be reported again.
