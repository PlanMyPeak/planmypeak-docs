For athletes

For athletes

Who can use this — any athlete can register and connect their own TrainingPeaks or Strava. If your coach already works with you in PlanMyPeak, sign up with the email they have on file so we can link you automatically.

Register your account

Open the PlanMyPeak app and choose Register as an athlete. Enter your first name and last name, your email address — if you train with a coach, use the one they have on file for you — and create a password of at least 8 characters.

Tick the box confirming you agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy — both open in a new tab so you don’t lose what you’ve typed. We record which version of each you agreed to, and when.

We’ll send a verification link to that email. Open it to confirm your address — this is how we make sure your account really belongs to you before connecting it to your training history.

Didn’t get the email? Check your spam folder first. If it still hasn’t arrived, try signing in — when your email isn’t confirmed yet, we show a Resend confirmation email button so you can request a fresh link.

If your coach already uses PlanMyPeak, sign up with the same email they have on file so we can link you to them automatically. Not sure which email that is? Ask your coach. You don’t need a coach to sign up — if there’s no match yet, your account is still ready to use and your coach can link to you later.

After you verify

Once you click the verification link, your account is ready. On your first visit we show a welcome page inviting you to Connect TrainingPeaks — connecting is the fastest way to get value from PlanMyPeak, because it brings your workouts in for review. You can choose Connect TrainingPeaks right there, or select Skip for now to go straight to the app.

Skipping is never final: you can connect at any time from Integrations, in your account menu (the avatar in the top-right) — you don’t have to wait for a coach either. When a coach later adds you to their roster, your existing connection links to them automatically. If you already connected TrainingPeaks, the welcome prompt simply doesn’t appear.

Your own connection is what syncs your workouts. Your coach connecting their TrainingPeaks account imports you onto their roster, but it does not pull in your workouts. Until you connect TrainingPeaks here yourself, your coach has nothing to analyze.

Returning to the app

When you reopen PlanMyPeak while still signed in, the app opens your athlete dashboard. Saved links to your calendar, settings, or a workout report continue to open those pages. If an old bookmark points to a coach page and you only have an athlete account, the app takes you to your athlete dashboard automatically.

If checking your account access fails, choose Try again when your connection returns. You can retry with your existing sign-in.

Connect your TrainingPeaks

Open Integrations from your account menu and find the TrainingPeaks card to link your TrainingPeaks account. Connecting lets PlanMyPeak read your completed and planned workouts so your coach’s analysis stays up to date — straight from your own TrainingPeaks account.

To connect:

  1. In Integrations, select the arrow on the TrainingPeaks card to expand it, then choose Connect TrainingPeaks.
  2. Sign in to TrainingPeaks and approve the access PlanMyPeak asks for.
  3. You’ll come back to Integrations with your status shown as Connected.

Once you’re connected, your recent and upcoming workouts start syncing — as long as a coach has added you to their roster. Until then, connecting links your account but no workouts are read, and Sync now tells you so. Syncing starts on its own the moment a coach adds you. If anything looks out of date after that, open Advanced — manual sync on the TrainingPeaks card and choose Sync now.

Use your own TrainingPeaks login — not your coach’s. It’s fine if your TrainingPeaks email is different from the email you signed up with; connecting links the two for you. If your coach already added you from TrainingPeaks under that other email, connecting also joins you to the profile your coach sees — see Connecting TrainingPeaks.

Each card starts collapsed to a single row. Expand it and you’ll see:

  • Last sync — when your workouts were last refreshed.
  • Token expires — when access needs renewing. We refresh it automatically.
  • Connected since — when you first linked the account.
  • A ? next to the provider name listing the exact scopes granted to PlanMyPeak.

The status pill always shows where you stand:

  • Connected — your workouts are syncing. You can Disconnect any time to stop sharing, or Reconnect to refresh access.
  • Re-authorization needed — your TrainingPeaks access expired. Choose Re-authorize to resume syncing.
  • Not connected — choose Connect TrainingPeaks to get started.

If TrainingPeaks doesn’t grant the access we need, we’ll tell you on the Integrations page and you can reconnect and accept the requested permissions.

Requested a sync before a coach found you? You’ll see “Sync will start once a coach adds you to their roster — nothing to sync into yet.” Your connection is fine — there’s just no coach to sync into. It starts on its own once a coach adds you.

Connect Strava

If your TrainingPeaks account isn’t Premium, TrainingPeaks doesn’t share the detailed second-by-second data the analysis needs. Connecting Strava fills that gap. It’s optional, read-only, and works the same way as the TrainingPeaks card — expand it, choose Connect Strava, approve access, and you’re done. See Connecting Strava for the full story.

Intervals.icu

The Integrations page also lists Intervals.icu, marked Coming soon. The connect button is deliberately disabled — we’re still building this one. Nothing to do here yet.

Your dashboard

The Dashboard is your home screen: a page of training numbers, top to bottom.

Your training state today. Four cards open the page, each with a line showing the last 28 days:

  • Fitness — how much training you’ve built up over the last several weeks, with the change since a week ago.
  • Fatigue — how much recent, short-term load you’re carrying, with the change since a week ago.
  • Form — the balance between the two. The card names the zone you’re in: Fresh (ready to perform), Grey zone (maintaining), Productive (building fitness) or High risk (fatigue well above fitness).
  • Resting HR 7D — your average resting heart rate over the last seven days, with today’s resting heart rate and sleep when they were recorded. If you have no resting heart rate in the last four weeks, this card isn’t shown.

When your account first connects, fitness numbers need a few weeks of history to settle, so they read Still calibrating — treat them as rough until then. If there isn’t enough synced history to work them out yet, the cards read Fitness unavailable instead; they fill in on their own once your history has been processed.

This week. How the current week is going against your plan: TSS, duration and sessions done out of what’s planned, plus the week’s distance and work, how much of your planned load you’ve completed over the last four full weeks (Compliance 28D — tap the ? beside it for how it’s counted), and your four-week average. Rest days and coach notes on your plan are not counted as sessions. Below that, the next week load check scores next week’s plan out of 100 against your recent training and says in one sentence whether it’s a sensible step or a big jump. Tap the ? beside it to see how the score is set. A much lighter week scores 80, which is fine when it’s a planned recovery week.

Today and Tomorrow. Your sessions for both days, each marked Done, Missed or To do, along with rest days and any notes from your coach. Open calendar takes you to the full calendar.

Weekly review. If your coach has turned on weekly reviews, a short preview of your latest review sits here, with Read full review to open it.

Fitness & form. A chart of fitness, fatigue and form over the last twelve weeks and about three weeks ahead. The part ahead is dashed: it’s projected from your planned workouts, assuming you complete them. If some planned sessions have no TSS, the chart says the projection leaves them out. Hover over (or tab to) the chart to read any day’s values.

Weekly load. Your last twelve weeks as bars: what you completed as a solid bar, what was planned as a dashed outline. Switch between TSS, Duration and Distance with the buttons above the chart.

The menu runs along the top of every page — Dashboard, Calendar and, if your coach has turned it on, Weekly Review — so your pages get the full width of the screen. On a phone the menu moves to its own row under the logo, with all three pages always named and in reach. Your assigned coach is shown on the right of the menu bar, beside your notifications. If you haven’t been matched with a coach yet, it reads Coach: not assigned. On a smaller screen the coach’s name is hidden to make room.

View your activities

Open Calendar in the menu at the top (it used to be called Reports) to see your workouts laid out week by week across the full width of the page. You don’t have to wait for your coach to finish a review to see them. Open one to see a quick review in place, or the full read-only view: your workout, its sensor data, and the analysis of how the effort matched the plan.

Your detected training plan

When your coach’s schedule for you follows one of their training plans, a Detected training plan card sits above the calendar and stays in view as you scroll. It shows:

  • the plan’s name and which week of it you are in, for example Week 6 of 12
  • who assigned it (your coach, or a head coach of your coach’s team) and the dates it started and ends
  • a strip with one segment per week of the plan: weeks behind you are filled, the current week is outlined, and weeks ahead are pale

The card appears only once the workouts on your TrainingPeaks calendar clearly match one of your coach’s plans, or one of their head coach’s plans. Until then there is no card.

If you change coach, the plan you’re following stays on the card. It ends only when its sessions are removed from your TrainingPeaks calendar or it reaches its last week.

Each workout counts for the plan on the day it was scheduled. Doing it a day early, a day late or after midnight doesn’t change your detected plan.

With a plan detected, your calendar also shows what the plan says:

  • a Plan note from your coach on a matching workout
  • a dashed Missing from calendar card for a planned session that isn’t on your calendar
  • Not synced yet cards for sessions beyond the dates we have synced so far. Those are not missed, they just haven’t been read from TrainingPeaks yet.
  • an amber Coach note card for each note in the plan, with the first few lines of the note. Click it to read the whole note.
  • a rest day row for a rest day in the plan

Sessions that are already on your TrainingPeaks calendar are not drawn a second time. A rest day appears once: as your TrainingPeaks rest day when it is there, otherwise from the plan. A rest day is never counted as missed. If you trained on a day the plan marks as rest, that day shows your workout and the rest day row reads Training recorded.

If your coach takes the plan off your TrainingPeaks calendar, the card and the plan’s notes and sessions disappear after the next sync; if they apply it again, they come back. Detection runs after each sync, so right after you connect it can take one sync before the card appears.

Resting heart rate and sleep

When your TrainingPeaks account records them, each past day shows two small pills beside the date:

  • a heart with that morning’s resting heart rate, for example 45 bpm
  • a moon with how long you slept, for example 7 h 02 min

They come from the wellness data you or your watch log in TrainingPeaks. Sleep shows on the day TrainingPeaks records it — normally the morning you woke up — and if more than one sleep is logged that day, the first one (usually your night’s sleep) is shown. A day with no reading simply shows nothing. New readings appear after the next sync, usually within a few minutes. On a smaller window the pills are hidden to leave room for your workouts.

TrainingPeaks only shares this wellness data for TrainingPeaks Premium accounts. On a basic account your days show no resting heart rate or sleep. If you upgrade to Premium, they appear after the next sync.

The weekly summary column

At the end of each week sits a summary of that week, headed with its number and dates — for example 2026-W41 and 5 - 11 Oct. The current week’s summary is highlighted. It shows:

  • CTL, ATL and TSB (your fitness, fatigue and form) at the end of the week, the Sunday.
    • For the current week they read end of week, projected: what they will be on Sunday if you complete the rest of the week as planned.
    • For a future week they read projected from plan.
    • A week after the last session in your plan shows dashes and No plan to project from.
    • Fitness unavailable means we don’t have enough of your training history to work these out yet. Once a completed week of fitness history is available, later weeks can show fitness even when earlier weeks in the chart are unavailable.
  • An Actual / Plan table of Sessions, Duration, TSS and Distance, with distance in the units you chose in your settings. A future week has nothing actual yet, so its actual column shows dashes.
  • For the current week and earlier ones, a bar showing what share of the planned TSS you completed, for example 86% of plan TSS.

Use Weekly summary in the toolbar to show or hide the column. It starts hidden on a smaller window, and the button works at any size.

Finding your way around the calendar

  • The calendar opens with last week at the top and the current week just below it. Opened from View on calendar in a feedback email, it opens on that day instead, outlined so you can spot it.
  • The toolbar stays at the top as you scroll. Its title shows the month of the first week on screen, for example October 2026, and changes as you scroll into another month.
  • The ‹ and › buttons move the calendar one week back or forward.
  • Today takes you back to the current week. Today’s date has a blue ring and a filled date, and earlier days are lightly shaded.
  • The legend next to the buttons explains the colours: Completed, Not completed, Planned and Coach note.
  • Load earlier activities at the top pulls in the weeks before the ones on screen; scrolling to the bottom loads later ones automatically. The calendar covers roughly a year either side of today.
  • Opening an activity shows its review in a pop-up. Use the Full review link in the pop-up’s header to open the complete review on its own page.

On a phone

On a phone the calendar becomes a list you scroll through. Each week is its own card, headed with its week number and dates, and each day is a row: the weekday and date on the left, that day’s workouts on the right as the same full cards you see on a computer. Today’s date is highlighted in blue.

Past days show your resting heart rate and sleep above that day’s workouts.

The calendar opens on today, and Today brings you back to today’s row. The month at the top and the arrows work the same way as on a computer. The colour legend is left out to save room; each card spells its status out instead. Weekly summary adds each week’s summary at the foot of its card.

If your coach has a training plan detected for you, its name, the week you’re in and its weeks bar stay pinned at the top of the calendar as you scroll. Who assigned it and its dates sit just above the calendar and scroll away.

Tap a workout to go to its full review page. Your browser’s back button brings you back to the calendar on that same day.

Reading an activity card

Each card shows an icon for the sport, the workout title and its DUR (duration), TSS and, when there is one, DIST (distance). A dash means we have no figure for it. A KEY tag marks a key session: a threshold, VO2max, sweet spot or anaerobic workout.

Until you complete a workout, its figures are what your coach prescribed. Once the session comes back from your watch or head unit, each figure reads what you did / what was planned, for example 1 h 08 min/1 h 10 min.

Strength sessions you log on a device. TrainingPeaks keeps strength sessions recorded on a watch or in a strength app separately from your other workouts. They appear on your calendar with their duration and whether you completed them, but their TSS always shows a dash. TrainingPeaks doesn’t share the training load of these sessions, so they add their time to your weekly totals but nothing to your TSS, fitness or form. They have no workout analysis or AI feedback either.

The card’s colour and its status line tell you how the session went:

  • Completed (green) — a planned workout you completed.
  • Unplanned (green) — a workout you did that wasn’t in your plan.
  • Not completed (red) — a planned workout whose day has passed without a matching activity.
  • To do today (blue) — a planned workout for today.
  • Planned (white) — a workout still to come.

A rest day is a quiet dashed row and is never flagged as missed. On a smaller window the sport icons are hidden and the figures stack, so all seven days still fit.

Open a card to read the full report. Its collapsed Workout Description panel holds your coach’s notes for the session, and when your coach wrote a Before you start note for that workout in their library it appears at the top of the panel, kept apart from the description.

Training context

Each activity review also shows Training context: a strip of day cards running from 2 days before, through the Day before and This activity (highlighted), to the Next day. Each activity shows its TSS and IF; where the workout was planned, the value reads planned → actual and TSS carries a chip with the difference from the plan. The next-day card also shows planned duration and a Planned chip, which becomes Completed once the workout is done. This appears on both the full page and the activity pop-up, including before AI feedback is approved. It is a panel you can fold away by tapping its heading; on a phone it starts folded, so tap Training context to open it.

When a day contains cycling, the card shows all cycling activities, including mountain-bike rides. Days without cycling show their other activities. When a previous day had several activities, they are combined into one summary with the number of rides, the day’s total TSS, and the day’s time-weighted IF; View all lists each ride with its own TSS and IF. Empty days are labelled No completed activity or Nothing planned; a prescribed rest day ahead is labelled Rest day (planned).

Progress comparison

When you’ve ridden the same planned session before, the review also shows Progress comparison — how this ride measures up against your earlier repeats of it. The numbers are there as soon as the ride is: headline changes in average power, heart rate, normalised power and decoupling against your own average for that session, a trend chart across every repeat, and a table of each one. The written verdict and coaching read-out appear once your coach has approved the report, along with the rest of their feedback.

Select the linked date on an earlier repeat to open it in the same activity popup as the calendar. Close the popup to return to the comparison you were reading. The row marked This ride is not a link because it is already open.

Saving the chart as an image

The review has an export button that turns the activity chart into a PNG you can save or share — handy for a training log or a message. You choose which data series to include and how the image is sized before downloading. The button is available on a computer or tablet, not on a phone.

Your activity, its data, and the compliance analysis are visible right away. The AI coach feedback is the one part that waits — until your coach approves it, that section shows a “waiting for coach review” (or “being prepared”) note instead. This keeps drafts and in-progress feedback private until they’re ready.

Activities are read-only for athletes — approving and editing feedback are coach-only actions. The one thing you can add is your own feedback on a reviewed report, below.

Print a report or save it as a PDF

You can print any of your reports, or save it as a PDF to keep in a training log.

Select the printer icon at the top of the activity page, or press Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac). In the print dialog, choose your printer, or pick Save as PDF (Chrome, Edge) / PDF → Save as PDF (Safari) / Print to File (Firefox) as the destination. The printer icon is available on a computer or tablet, not on a phone.

The printed report is the whole page — the chart, your numbers, and the coach feedback — over as many pages as it needs. It prints on a light background even if you use the dark theme, keeps the colours that mean something (training zones, compliance), and doesn’t split a card across two pages. The workout description is included in full even if you left it collapsed, while the step-by-step breakdown of the planned workout beside it is left off. Buttons, menus, help links, and the chart’s smoothing slider are left off the page too.

Only what you can see prints. If your coach hasn’t approved the AI feedback yet, the printed report shows the same “waiting for coach review” note that the page does.

Answer a clarifying question

Sometimes the AI coach can’t tell from the data alone what happened — for example, whether a stop in the middle of a hard interval was something external or a sign that fatigue got the better of the effort. When that’s the case, the coach feedback ends with a Clarifying Questions block listing the possible explanations.

Tap the explanation that fits. The option shows a loading indicator for a moment while the coach works out what your answer means; then the recommendation that follows from it appears under the option, and your coach sees your answer on their side of the same report. Only your chosen option shows its recommendation, so you answer with what happened, not with the outcome you would prefer. You can change your answer later by tapping a different option. Nothing in your training plan changes automatically — the answer tells your coach which recommendation applies, and the compliance score still reflects the work you actually completed.

If none of the listed explanations fit, choose Something else and write a short note about what happened (up to 500 characters), then save it. Your coach reads the note before deciding what, if anything, to change. You can edit the note later, or switch back to one of the listed explanations.

Sometimes the question mentions your recent training: if you completed noticeably more training load than planned over the seven days before the session, it will say roughly how much you did against how much was planned and ask whether that could be the reason. It’s a genuine question, not a verdict — answer “No, something interrupted me” if that’s what happened, and your targets stay as they are. Those two totals are the only numbers shown; the question never lists your rides or your plan.

Not every report has a question. It appears only on indoor rides, only when there’s genuinely something the data can’t settle, and only once the report has been reviewed.

Log how the session felt

Numbers only tell half the story of a ride. On any completed activity, a How It Felt card lets you record the half only you can:

  • Effort (RPE) — how hard the session felt, on a slider from 1 (very easy) to 10 (all I had). Drag it, or focus it and use the arrow keys. Whatever you land on is named as well as numbered (“7 · Hard”), so you’re picking a description rather than guessing at a scale.
  • Feel — how you felt doing it: Very weak, Weak, Normal, Good, or Strong.

The two are independent — log one, both, or neither. Tap the feel that’s already highlighted to clear it; the other half stays as it was. Clear in the top corner empties both.

Each choice saves on its own — there’s no Save button. If a save fails, the card says so and keeps your entry so you can try again.

This is available as soon as the activity is recorded, so you can log it straight after the ride. It doesn’t wait for your coach to review anything.

The email that tells you your feedback is ready also has a How did it feel? section with the 1-10 effort scale. Tap anywhere in it — any number, the text, or Log how it felt — and the activity opens straight at the How It Felt card with the effort slider ready. Nothing is saved from the email itself; you choose your values on the page. If you’re signed out, you’ll land on the card right after signing in.

If your connected account already has an effort or feel recorded for the activity, it shows up here labelled From your connected account. Setting your own value replaces it, and clearing yours brings the connected value back — nothing you enter is ever overwritten by a later sync.

Your coach sees both values on their view of the same activity, and can’t change them.

Give feedback on a report

Once a report is reviewed, a Your Feedback panel appears under the coach feedback:

  • Thumbs up / thumbs down — tap to tell your coach whether the report was helpful. Tap again to clear it, or switch to the other thumb any time.
  • Comment — leave a note about how the session actually felt. The comment box starts small and grows when you tap into it. You can add more comments later; they build up as a conversation under the report.

Your coach sees your rating and comments on their side of the same report, so it’s a quick way to close the loop after each session.

If your TrainingPeaks account is connected and the activity started within the last 24 hours, each comment is also posted to the matching workout in TrainingPeaks — so your note travels with the workout wherever your coach reads it. This needs a TrainingPeaks account type that supports workout comments (Premium); if the post doesn’t go through for any reason, your comment still appears here as normal. Comments on older activities stay in the app.

The feedback panel only appears once the report has been reviewed — you can’t rate a report you can’t read yet. Comments can’t be edited or deleted after sending.

Get notified when feedback is ready

When your coach approves a report for an activity that started within the last 24 hours, you’re notified — no need to keep checking back:

  • In the app — the bell in the top bar shows a count of unread notifications. Open it to see “Your activity feedback is ready” and select it to jump straight to that report. Use Mark all read to clear the count.
  • By email — we also email you your coach’s feedback. The email includes the same words posted as a comment on your TrainingPeaks workout, so you can read it without leaving your inbox, plus a View feedback link to the full report.

The 24-hour limit is checked when each message is sent. Older activities still have their reports and approved feedback in the app, but do not create activity notifications, feedback emails, or TrainingPeaks comments. This also applies when an approval or message is delayed. Weekly reviews and account messages follow their own notification settings.

What the feedback email shows

Besides your coach’s feedback, the email gives you the essentials at a glance:

  • How the session went — the workout’s date with its TSS, IF (intensity factor) and duration. When the workout was planned, each figure also shows what was planned, for example 74, planned 72.
  • Up next — what’s coming after that workout:
    • Each day without a workout is listed as a Rest day, up to your next scheduled workout.
    • Your next workout shows its name, planned duration, TSS and IF, a small chart of its intervals, and a short description. The chart is coloured by your own TrainingPeaks power zones, matching the activity chart in the app; until those zones have synced, it uses standard training zones.
    • View on calendar opens your calendar on that day.
    • If no workout is planned in the next week, the email says so. If your calendar is empty, the email leaves this section out.

Up next starts from the day after the workout — or from today, if your coach reviewed it a few days later.

Feedback reviews are not medical advice. Talk to a coach before making any changes to your plan. You’ll see this reminder at the end of each activity review and in your feedback and weekly review emails.

In-app notifications refresh every few minutes while you have the app open, so a new one may take a moment to appear. Reload the page if you want to check straight away — the email arrives independently.

Prefer fewer emails? Open Settings and turn off Feedback ready emails under Notifications — the email’s Manage your notification preferences link takes you there. You’ll still see the in-app notification.

Your settings

Open Settings from your account menu — the avatar in the top-right. Everything lives on one page:

  • Your details — your first name, last name, and timezone. Your timezone makes sure your weekly training lines up with your local week. Select Save changes when you’re done.
  • Measurements — choose whether distance shows in kilometres or miles, elevation in metres or feet, and temperature in Celsius or Fahrenheit. Your choice applies throughout the app and to newly generated reports. Select Save measurements.
  • Notifications — the Feedback ready emails switch described above.

Measurements are yours. Your coach has their own setting, so you can read the same workout in miles while they read it in kilometres — neither choice changes the other’s view, and no data is converted or lost.

Light and dark themes

The sun / moon button in the top bar switches between light and dark. Your choice is remembered for next time.

Signing in later

Use Sign in with your email and password any time to return to your reports.
